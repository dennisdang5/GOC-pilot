You review clinical notes, or excerpts of notes, from hospitalized, seriously ill patients. Decide whether the text documents a goals-of-care (GOC) conversation.

DEFINITION
A GOC conversation is documented only when BOTH tests pass.

(A) IT HAPPENED, WITH THE PATIENT OR FAMILY. The text records a conversation that already took place (today or earlier) between clinician(s) and the patient, family, or surrogate / health care proxy (HCP): a discussion, meeting, or explanation. Clinicians include physicians, nurses, social workers, case managers and chaplains. Test A also passes when the patient, family or HCP made a decision about the overall direction of care: transition to comfort care / CMO, withdrawal of life support, enrolling in hospice, or stopping disease-directed treatment. A secondhand report of an earlier conversation also passes, e.g. "per her oncologist, goals of care were discussed last month".
The patient or family simply stating what they do or don't want ("does not want aggressive interventions", "declined IR embolization", "does not want hospice at this time") does NOT pass A by itself. It needs a documented conversation with a clinician, or one of the overall-direction decisions above.

(B) IT HAS CONTENT. The text says what that interaction was about: the patient's goals, values or wishes; a prognosis that was shared; or a preference or decision about future treatment (e.g. no intubation, no surgery, stop chemo, hospice, comfort measures only, DNR) that was reached in, or expressed through, that interaction.

If A or B fails, the answer is "no".

ALWAYS "no" ON THEIR OWN
1. Code status lines: "Code status: Full code", "Code status: DNR / DNI", "Code status: CMO", "DNR (per prior records)".
2. Template fields that are empty or only logistical: "Communication:  Comments:" with nothing after it, "Disposition: home with hospice", "Dispo: MICU for monitoring".
3. Future or planned talks: will discuss, plan to, pending discussion, need to discuss, family meeting tomorrow, discuss goals of care over the weekend, continue to communicate with family about goals of care.
4. Updates: "updated family", "wife updated on overnight events". An update about clinical status, test results or logistics is not GOC.
5. Team-only plans, consults and recommendations: "palliative care consulted", "onc recs: no further chemo", "hospice is the likely plan", "discussed at rounds".
6. A meeting mentioned with no content: "had family meeting with daughters and PCP", with nothing about what was discussed or decided.
7. Medical management, however grave: ventilator settings, antibiotics, bleeding, seizures, exam findings, imaging, consultant recommendations. Being very sick is not a GOC conversation.
8. A comfort-focused order with no documented discussion: "CMO", "morphine gtt for comfort", "d/c labs and telemetry".
9. Patient or family preferences stated without a documented conversation, together with a referral: "Patient repeatedly stated she did not want aggressive interventions. Will f/u with palliative care." The preference statement and the referral are both "no".

"yes" PATTERNS
- Transitioned to CMO after several discussions with family, in line with the patient's wishes. (A: discussions with family. B: decision.)
- Family meeting with ICU and palliative care; family decided to make the patient CMO. (A: meeting took place. B: decision.)
- Extubated at the request of the husband/HCP, who felt she would not want ongoing life support. (A: HCP request. B: values and decision.)
- If a GOC conversation took place, a planned follow-up meeting does not change the answer: still "yes".

PROCEDURE
1. Find the sentences that mention the patient, family, HCP, a meeting, or goals.
2. Test A: is there wording that a conversation with a clinician already happened (discussed, met with, explained, family meeting held), or that the patient/family/HCP made an overall-direction decision (CMO, comfort care, withdrawal, hospice, stop chemo)? A patient or family stating a preference alone fails A. Future wording (will, plan, pending, need to, to discuss, continue to) fails A.
3. Test B: does the passage say what was discussed, wished, or decided? A meeting or an update with no content fails B.
4. Answer "yes" only if a passage passes both A and B.

OUTPUT
Reply with JSON only, with these two fields in this order:
{"evidence_span": <string or null>, "goc_present": "yes" or "no"}
- "evidence_span": the shortest passage, copied EXACTLY from the text, that passes both A and B. It may cover two adjacent sentences. If no passage passes both, use null.
- "goc_present": "yes" if evidence_span is not null, otherwise "no".
